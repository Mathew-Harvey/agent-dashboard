# 2026-02-26 - Automation Products People Pay For

## Key Findings

### Reddit Discussions Research

**1. "$5k/month Automation Side Project" (r/SideProject)**
- Started by solving simple problems for local businesses
- First client: gym needing automated follow-up messages
- Then: dentist's office, real estate agent
- Key insights:
  - Solve one annoying problem for someone you know
  - Keep tech simple so people actually use it
  - Charge for ongoing support instead of one-time builds
  - Watch their real workflow instead of what they describe
- Quote: "It is wild how much demand there is for basic automation. People just want less friction."
- **Pattern: B2B local service automation**

**2. "$600/month Passive Income - Cold Email Automation" (r/IndieHackers)**
- Built: ColdConvert - automated cold email outreach system
- Combined: automated lead sourcing, deliverability safeguards, always-on sending
- Result: ~$600/month true passive income, 3 new clients per month
- Key: Built for own needs first, then productized

**3. "$2,800 in 2 Months - Buildpad" (r/IndieHackers)**
- Product: Buildpad - not automation but validated idea first
- Key lesson: Validate before building, track metrics

**4. "20 Startup Ideas - Automation Combinations" (r/IndieHackers)**

### Patterns That Work

1. **Solve boring problems for small businesses**
   - Follow-up messages
   - Appointment reminders
   - Simple CRM integrations
   
2. **Build for yourself first, productize second**
   - Cold email system → sold to others
   - Personal automation → client automation

3. **Ongoing support > one-time builds**
   - Recurring revenue model
   - Simpler tech = easier to maintain

4. **Look for repetitive tasks in ignored places**
   - Local businesses have huge gaps
   - No flashy tech needed
